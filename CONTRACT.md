# CONTRACT.md — Cradle

Cradle is an owned replacement for Airtable, Smartsheet and Quickbase: relational tables with formulas, lookups and rollups, attachments, many views, an interface builder, automations, an API and permissions, so a team runs its operations on it. This contract describes **the product we are building towards**, not only what ships today; every clause carries its acceptance evidence and its current status, and the [status table](#status) at the end shows how close Cradle is. A clause marked `partial` or `missing` is a binding target for later build rounds, not a claim about the current release. The retained [Launch readiness](#included-application-launch-readiness) clauses are one included application built on the same records platform; repair navigation is disabled unless the owner enables it (APP-SCOPE-001).

**Buyer.** A US operations lead at a small or mid-size business (10–500 people) who runs inventory, vendors, requests, approvals, assets, events or field work in Airtable, Smartsheet or Quickbase, or in a tangle of spreadsheets, and wants the whole app (data, views, interfaces, automations, permissions) in an installation they own, priced in USD with no per-seat license. US norms apply by default: USD currency, US date display with ISO 8601 storage and export (ACT-003), US federal holidays in date functions, E.164 phone storage with US formatting, and A2P 10DLC for SMS.

**Switching bar.** A buyer of any named incumbent can move in (MIGRATE-001 to MIGRATE-004) and find the job they bought: records and calculations (FIELD, CALC, FILE), views and collaboration (VIEW, COLLAB), forms, interfaces and reporting (FORM, IFACE, REPORT), roles and sharing (PERM), automations and follow-up (AUTO-USER), API and integrations (API-USER, WEBHOOK-USER, MCP, INTEG), sync and recovery (SYNC, SNAP) and mobile (MOBILE). Beyond today's up-and-comers (Baserow, NocoDB, Grist), agents build, test and operate the app under the owner's control (AGENT).

**Reading a clause.** Each clause states the behavior a buyer gets, its *Acceptance* (the journey or test through a public door, meaning the operator UI at `/app`, a public form or portal, `/api/v1`, MCP, the CLI, or the emitted Worker's queue and schedule, that proves it) and its *Status*: `done` (implemented and exercised by the cited test), `partial` (some of the behavior is implemented, cited, and the rest is named) or `missing`. Fakes are allowed only at the paid or network edge. Where a retained clause speaks of capabilities the product "ships" (for example HOME-002's landing copy), it means clauses whose status is `done`.

Security, privacy and operational guarantees are in `BASELINE.md`. No product clause supersedes a baseline requirement; a future exception must name the exact `BASE-` ID and its owner-accepted replacement meaning and consequences. Capacity and rate limits are in `seed.json → limits`; times are p95 at those limits. Clauses marked *Policy* have a default that buyers may replace through the named extension point, subject to the clause's explicit limits. `seed.json` describes the current release, including non-goals that some clauses here retire (public and embeddable views, sync, notification channels beyond in-app and email, native mobile apps, bounded scripts); each non-goal is removed from `seed.json` in the build round that delivers the clause replacing it. Default verification (`rer verify`) runs the local application suites within the 200-test seed budget; local verification alone does not prove hosted readiness.

## Automations and follow-up

Automations do work without someone starting it each time, and follow-up keeps work moving until the record's outcome is reached. AUTO-001 to AUTO-011 (Launch readiness section) govern the launch template's shipped automations; AUTO-USER clauses govern automations on every base.

- **AUTO-USER-001 — Approved record triggers.** An automation author (AUTO-USER-009 names who: base owners and creators) with current app and record access can create, edit, disable and renew created/updated record triggers with ordered notify, email, update, webhook and Slack actions. An enabled automation runs for the lifetime AUTO-USER-009 sets: until disabled or until its author's authority lapses, with any expiry being an owner-set policy announced in advance. Each committed native change captures the enabled definition versions atomically. AUTO retains run/action history and the approved definition snapshot. Disable cancels queued work; lost issuer, app, subject or credential authority stops new effects, and an operator recipient who loses authority stops receiving that effect. Email to addresses that are not operators (customers, vendors, requesters) needs no recipient authority, so removing an operator stops only the sends addressed to that operator (AUTO-USER-010). Repeated queue delivery preserves one logical run/effect. A repeated automation or excessive causal depth is recorded as Suppressed. External uncertainty is visible and never authorizes automatic resend.
  *Acceptance:* Automation tests on emitted workerd; browser journey for definition and run history.
  *Status:* done — tests/user-automations.mjs; tests/ui-user-automations.mjs; src/domains/automations/capture.server.ts. Capture, history, disabling, fencing on lost authority and suppression are done. Today only the installation owner authors, with approval capped at 30 days (src/domains/authority/user-delegation.server.ts:48). Creator authorship and the run-until-disabled lifetime are tracked under AUTO-USER-009. Email to addresses that are not operators is tracked under AUTO-USER-010.

### Fields, calculations and attachments

The data model an Airtable, Smartsheet or Quickbase buyer already relies on: rich field types, formulas, lookups and rollups that stay correct as linked records change, and files stored with the record.

- **FIELD-001 — Complete field types.** A table can hold:
  - single-line text;
  - long text, plain or rich, where rich text supports bold, italic, strikethrough, inline code, headings (levels 1–3), bulleted and numbered lists, checklists, block quotes, code blocks and links, stored as Markdown;
  - number (integer, or decimal with set precision);
  - currency (USD by default, any ISO 4217 code, stored as exact minor units);
  - percent;
  - date, and date-time with time zone, where each date-time field displays either in each viewer's local time zone or in one fixed time zone for everyone (the workspace zone by default), stores UTC and shows the zone it used;
  - duration;
  - checkbox;
  - single select and multiple select, with colors;
  - rating (1–10);
  - email;
  - US-first phone (stored E.164, shown (555) 123-4567);
  - URL;
  - user (one or many operators; notification on assignment is FIELD-004);
  - linked record (one or many; its record picker is FIELD-005);
  - lookup, rollup, count and formula;
  - attachment;
  - barcode (scanned or typed, a Code 128, UPC, EAN or QR value);
  - autonumber;
  - created time, last-modified time, created by and last-modified by;
  - button (IFACE-004).

  Each type validates on every door (UI, form, API, import, automation) and exports and re-imports losslessly.

## Included application: Launch readiness

Launch readiness is a complete application template on the records platform (campaigns, deliverables, milestones, risks, decisions, saved lenses, shipped automations, notifications and read-only AI analysis). Its clauses keep their data and security semantics. Some rules here are workspace-wide and apply to every base: sign-in (AUTH-BASE-001), operator management, time zone, removal, erasure and currency (OPS-002 to OPS-006), operator views (VIEW-001 to VIEW-007), dates and times (ACT-003) and capacity limits (DATA-003). OPS-001's equal-operator rule covers only the launch template; every other base uses roles and rules (PERM-001 to PERM-003). Clauses without a clause-tagged test are marked partial until a test through a public door names them.

### Words this contract uses

- **Operator** — a person with a session, invited under OPS-002. Every actor in this contract is an operator or an automation run.
- **Active** and **archived** — a campaign's two lifecycle states (CAMP-002). **Non-archived** always means active.
- **Open** and **resolved** — a risk's two conditions (RISK-002). **Unresolved** and **terminal** are the equivalents for a decision (DEC-001).
- **Run** — one logical execution of an automation, keyed by automation, definition version, and trigger event or scheduled instant. An **attempt** is one transport delivery inside a run (AUTO-003).
- **Derived** — a value computed from linked records on read. Derived values are never written directly; a request that writes one is rejected under `BASE-INPUT-001`.

### Operators

- **AUTH-BASE-001 — Install and sign in.** A new installation uses Base operator-auth to atomically bootstrap its configured first owner on an empty auth store using a deployment setup token. Mail and an existing owner session are not prerequisites. Base owns opaque credentials, one-use magic-link confirmation, active operator/version checks and host/origin admission. The setup ledger cannot be reset by replacing the token. Base mail explicitly selects the RER Managed relay, SendGrid or Resend; failure never selects a fallback provider or claims mailbox delivery. Cradle maps admitted identities to its existing product IDs and current app/row/field policy, and prepared mutations also fence Base session revocation. A fresh local owner runs `rer dev`, which establishes a real Base dev session through the checkout key; optional `rer setup` writes private credentials with mode 0600. The signed-in owner completes durable first-run details at `/setup` unless installation configuration already set them (SET-003). The sign-in form fills its email field from `?login_hint=` when that is a complete email address; the deployment specification declares this (`surfaces.app.signIn`) so RER's Open app sends the claimed owner there with their email. Standalone local and hosted installs expose no captured-mail inbox or outbox page.
