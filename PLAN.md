# Cradle flagship plan

**6 October 2026 — records-first direction:** the records builder leads; launch readiness is the included template and repair navigation is opt-in. This decision supersedes the launch-first identity and Today-first home in the dated plan below. It preserves all retained data, policy, security and wider acceptance obligations. The canonical home is `/app`, leading to Records. See docs/contract-transition.md.

**29 September 2026 — rendering migration candidate.** The current implementation
uses TanStack Start documents and generated functions with explicit Hono HTTP
families in one emitted Worker. The 71 applied migration files are preserved.
This is work package 0 only; retained later feature code is not accepted flagship
or UX completion. [Document rendering](docs/document-rendering.md) records the
ownership boundary, commands, local evidence and remaining limitations.


The roadmap extends the existing Cradle product. Its launch portfolio, persisted
records and services are a starting point for the wider records builder and
optional operations applications. The roadmap is a set of obligations and
experiments, not a declaration that the general engine or complete operational
outcome is accepted.

## Product outcome

The next extension is an **owner-owned expert operation**: continually understand
the business, discover better portfolio strategies, build the tools they need,
and carry chosen strategies to measured outcomes. The [frontier direction](docs/frontier-direction.md)
defines seven required extensions at demonstrated scope and optional coordination
with neighbouring products. Records are grounded memory, editable apps are its
instruments, and the existing loops provide execution. This is proposed scope,
not current capability or authority.

Repairs and launch readiness are proving applications, not the category limit.
An owner must be able to author materially different operational models and
role-specific apps under shared identity and authority, connect common entities
across them, and make ordinary source changes when generated forms or rules no
longer fit. A held-out third owner-defined application is part of flagship
acceptance; re-skinning either initial app does not prove a general product.

Cradle helps an owner turn an operation and the work they already have into an
inspectable application, then keep the operation moving toward evidenced
outcomes. A capable agent can assemble the data model, screens, rules and
workflows; people can inspect and evaluate that candidate before it reaches live
data. During operation, software gathers evidence, proposes structured changes,
coordinates authorized work, detects exceptions, records outcomes, and offers
measured improvements to the process and the application itself.

The product retains a capable relational records experience underneath that
operating layer: typed records and links, stable fields, forms and views,
formulas and rollups, bulk changes, search, attachments, history, collaboration,
access control, import/export, declared integrations, and responsive mobile
work. It is useful as a conventional structured-records product even when no AI
provider is configured. AI construction or orchestration must not be required for
ordinary reads, edits, search, reporting, import/export, or already-authorized
deterministic work.

The intended buyer is an owner or operations lead in a small or mid-sized
organization whose important process spans email/files, locations or assets,
people and vendors, decisions, and another authoritative business system. The
40-store repair operation in [the workflow map](docs/workflows.md) is a concrete
test case, not yet a validated segment choice. The value hypothesis is less
total human work across every role, elapsed time and owner attention to reach and keep a correct product
at a stated quality floor, not simply faster app generation or more automation.

## The experience to prove

Start with the [eight user journeys](docs/user-journeys.md). The owner supplies
an outcome, existing sources and business constraints. Cradle prepares the model,
mappings, roles, connections, typed actions, measures and checks, then asks for
business authority in a plain “How I'll handle this” card. Proposed sensible
limits are not active grants. Authentication, provider consent, missing secrets
and required financial decisions remain explicit human steps. Source content
cannot grant permissions. Reuse answers and retrieve permitted facts before
asking the smallest useful question of the person who knows.

The default **Today** view contains **Needs your decision**, **Being handled**
and **Finished**. It is available when useful, not a mandatory daily checking
ritual. Domain apps, records, search, edits and lenses remain directly available;
chat is optional. Each summary shows the recommendation, current status, impact,
evidence freshness and coverage, important unknowns and next step. Open the case
for reasons and evidence, then optional technical/authoring detail. Never bury
exact spending, data egress, access changes, new commitments or material
uncertainty needed to authorize an action.

Routine work under valid grants is quiet. Group nonurgent meaningful updates at
an optional cadence; deduplicate urgent decisions and send them to the responsible
person. Remind only when due or escalation state changes under the existing
notification rules. Material outages remain visible and reach the responsible
person; this quiet-default design cannot suppress required critical alerts or
silently replace current `NOTIFY` delivery/muting semantics. No daily all-clear
spam, model picker, evaluation dataset, grant matrix or retry console is required
to run the business. Cradle handles novel work when competent and authorized;
uncertainty first prompts useful investigation, not automatic owner escalation.

## Why this direction

The supplied market analysis argues that structured-records software is evolving
as AI-assisted construction, enrichment and record access spread. Its central
product insight is that structured operational state remains useful even as the
builder or interface changes. Current official documentation shows construction,
record operations and AI workflow features across Airtable, Microsoft, Quickbase,
SmartSuite and Baserow, and code portability through Lovable. Generic app
generation, chat access and open-source code ownership are therefore weak
standalone distinctions. The observed claims, product maturity and limits are
separated in [the dated research synthesis](docs/research.md).

Cradle's hypothesis is that a stronger product combines owner-controlled source
and updates with dependable cross-system execution, durable evidence,
human-issued authority, recovery from uncertain outcomes, and low ongoing owner
attention. Competitors' public documentation is not proof that their workflows
fail, and our direction is not evidence of a moat. We must compare end-to-end
work and cost against a capable current platform and a capable agent building
from scratch at the same quality floor.

## Two first-class applications

1. **Operations built around structured records.** The flagship must support a
   complete user-designed relational application and one fully operated business
   workflow. Its initial exemplar is multi-site equipment repair: email/photo
   intake, asset resolution, contractor eligibility, quote comparison, the
   A$2,000 approval boundary, scheduling, completion evidence, store acceptance,
   and invoice handoff to the accounting system. Accounting remains authoritative
   for payment. The branch cases and screen behavior are specified in
   [workflows](docs/workflows.md).
2. **Launch and campaign readiness.** Cradle retains a first-class launch
   application with campaigns, deliverables, milestones and dependencies, risks,
   decisions, lenses, activity, collaboration, and the workflows promised by its
   current contract. It keeps its own navigation, concepts, dependencies, rules
   and acceptance; the flagship must complete its retained obligations. Shared
   record capabilities may support it, but it is not reduced to a template, a
   demo, or source history. The current thin UI/data implementation is not yet
   full contract acceptance.

The [continuing-operation and improvement loops](docs/operating-loops.md) define
how an owner sets objectives and grants, supervises durable work, and adopts
evaluated changes from recurring operational evidence. Both are required flagship
capabilities, beyond application construction and event-driven automation.

## Product invariants

- Preserve evidence, extracted claims, proposed changes, committed state,
  decisions, action attempts and receipts as distinct linked facts. An email or
  attachment can inform a record but cannot grant authority.
- Enforce row, field and action rules in server-side policy at every entry point:
  product UI, HTTP API, MCP, imports, integrations, scheduled work and queues.
  A filtered picker or hidden button is never the security boundary.
- Use typed business actions with named preconditions, scope and effects. A model
  cannot write arbitrary fields or call arbitrary endpoints.
- Treat model output and material supplied to a model as untrusted input. Bound
  provider context, external data and spending by feature, app, operator and
  action. Show an honest unconfigured or unavailable state; deterministic work
  continues.
- Allow routine work to proceed under an owner-issued standing grant without a
  click at each step. Grants name the actor, action, records or conditions,
  limits, expiry and revocation path. Preserve human decisions and separation of
  duties at real authority boundaries, including repair approval over A$2,000.
- Persist execution attempts and effects durably. Use concurrency control,
  idempotency keys and reconciliation. A lost response can mean **unknown**;
  never claim exactly-once external execution or retry a possibly successful
  action as if failure were known.
- Keep application source, schema, policies, UI and infrastructure fully
  owner-editable. Hooks, configuration, a generated DSL, or a universal runtime
  plugin system must not limit ordinary engineering changes.
- Generate or propose changes in an isolated candidate. Before live adoption,
  expose schema and permission diffs, backfill/replay effects, tests, recovery,
  and release identity. Existing installations retain applied migration history.

## Measurement targets and hypotheses

The baseline is currently **unmeasured**. Establish it with owners before
claiming improvement. Retain each numerator, denominator, exclusions and
measurement window; report repair complexity and provider/connector failure
separately.

| Measure | Planned target / decision rule | Denominator and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contract behavior | 100% of accepted, applicable clauses pass a named observable check; uncovered clauses are visible and cannot be counted as satisfied. | Each clause in the released `CONTRACT.md` and `BASELINE.md`; test and acceptance report at that release identity. |
| Access and authority | Zero unauthorized reads or actions in the defined cross-surface matrix; no hidden or filtered controls counted as enforcement. | Every declared row/field/action rule tested through UI, API, MCP, import, and background execution; include allowed and denied cases. |
| Evidence-backed operation | 100% of external action attempts end as a provider-confirmed receipt or an explicit, age-stamped unknown/failure requiring reconciliation. | Every action attempt in the fault matrix and every live sandbox attempt; no silent success inferred from a timeout. |
| Structured extraction | For low-impact descriptive fields eligible for standing auto-apply, at least 98% exact correctness in an independently adjudicated set of at least 500 examples per field class; ambiguous cases stay proposed or require review. | Correctly extracted and applied values / all eligible applied values, with unknown, ambiguous and rejected examples reported separately. Money, identity, authority, quote, completion and payment facts need risk-appropriate corroboration or human decision; model confidence never grants authority. This is a target, not current accuracy. |
| Repair workflow safety | Zero duplicate external commitments and zero spend approvals without the required authority in all deterministic fault and replay scenarios. | Every generated, retried, cancelled and reconciled scenario in the test suite, then all recorded live attempts; simulated and live-sandbox evidence remain separate. |
| Owner effort | Minimum hypothesis, not an ambition ceiling: at least 30% lower median active operator minutes per completed-and-accepted repair after workflow onboarding, while maintaining quality and no increase in unauthorized or duplicate effects. | Initial gate: replay at least 100 historical jobs and measure at least 30 prospective jobs, with complexity and missing-data caveats. Longitudinal validation: at least 100 completed repairs before and 100 after over comparable 90-day windows. Measure build, operate and maintain time separately; technical release need not wait for the longitudinal sample. |
| Application construction | End-to-end effort to describe, inspect, test and change a maintained-path app is lower than both alternatives at the same quality floor. | Matched task brief, explicit feature/quality floor, owner/agent elapsed time, corrections, integrations, deployment, recovery and later change for (a) Cradle path, (b) capable incumbent, (c) capable agent from scratch. |
| Ongoing economics | Report dollars per verified completed operation and cumulative three-year cost, including build, operation and maintenance; continue the maintained path only when it saves total owner work and has credible lower total cost or a clearly valued ownership benefit. | Provider/runtime/storage/integration charges plus agent and human hours, correction/recovery costs, per accepted repair and annual/cumulative totals. Current amount unmeasured. |
| Responsiveness | At the 40-store target profile below, p95 collection/detail and simple committed mutations stay under 1 second; filtered lens queries stay under 2 seconds. Retain the stricter current contract targets: global search p95 under 500ms (`VIEW-005`) and lens preview within 2 seconds (`LENS-002`). A new target does not relax a legacy clause. | Profile: 40 stores, 2,000 assets, 500 contractors, 10,000 historical plus 1,000 active work orders, up to 22,000 quotes, 100 users and 40 concurrent sessions. Measure at least 1,000 requests per operation after warmup, with fixed plan/runtime, data volume, concurrency and error rate. Also test current `seed.json` limits. External provider latency is separate. Target, not current measurement. |

These thresholds are acceptance targets, not historical performance or guaranteed
benefits. If measurement shows they are misaligned with owner needs, record a
deliberate contract change with its evidence; never weaken an existing clause or
safety obligation to improve a speed or no-touch metric.

### Attention acceptance hypothesis

For a routine repair with sufficient evidence and actual grants, require zero
owner dispatch/coordination touches; store staff submit the fault once and make
one unavoidable physical acceptance decision for a successful visit. Contractor
work and evidence, and any accounting review, still count as human work. A quote
above A$2,000 adds one prepared decision for the designated manager; changed
quotes and rejected work require renewed decisions, never artificial touch caps.
Check these role-specific budgets alongside the 30% minimum hypothesis and all
quality floors. This is a planned gate, not a measured saving.

### Operation scorecard

All measures below are **unmeasured**. Use UTC event clocks, stable case/action
identities and a declared reporting window, app/process/model version and case
complexity cohort. Report live, sandbox, historical replay and shadow separately.
Exclude test traffic and confirmed duplicate intake from live denominators, with
counts and reasons; never exclude failed or abandoned cases simply to improve a
metric. Retain raw counts, missing evidence and right-censored open cases. Compare
matched before/after cohorts using the same definitions; targets are owner-set
before evaluation. These measures feed [both loops](docs/operating-loops.md).

| Measure | Definition, evidence and exclusions |
| --- | --- |
| Correction rate | Distinct committed changes subsequently corrected for error / all committed changes in the originating-change cohort, observed through a declared follow-up cutoff. Link correction to original change, actor, reason and evidence; report late/unadjudicated corrections separately. Exclude ordinary new-fact updates and preference changes, with reason; do not exclude agent mistakes. |
| Exception rate | Distinct admitted cases entering a named blocked/review/unknown/stall exception / all admitted cases in the intake cohort through the cutoff. Case/event history supplies first occurrence and cause; also report episodes per case and ordinary required approvals separately so routine approval is not mislabeled failure. |
| Recovery success | Fault episodes restored to a verified valid state with reconciled effects / all fault episodes opened in the window, with a fixed follow-up cutoff. Attempts/receipts and independent verification supply evidence. Open, unrecoverable and manual recoveries stay visible; report automated/manual success separately. Repeated attempts are not new successful episodes. |
| Total active human minutes | Sum active onboarding, source preparation, physical inspection/work, approval, clarification, correction, monitoring and recovery minutes across owner, manager, store staff, contractor and accounting roles. Report each role and build/operate/maintain phase separately, including work transferred to another person or outside Cradle; unobserved time is missing, never zero. Retain failed, open, cancelled and abandoned cases in intake-cohort totals alongside per-verified-outcome costs. |
| Owner interruptions and repeated work | Owner interruptions / independently verified outcomes, alongside raw counts and all-case denominators. Count repeated requests for the same facts, repeated entry and human reconstruction of context per role/case; include onboarding and unresolved requests. Report non-owner interruption totals so routing work away from the owner cannot masquerade as removal. |
| Operator touch count | Attributed consequential human interventions per case (edit/correction, approval, clarification, reconciliation, takeover); report total and median/p95 for completed and open intake cohorts separately. Audit/UI action IDs deduplicate retries and group one submitted transaction as one touch. Passive views and machine actions excluded; time spent and uninstrumented external work reported alongside rather than assumed zero. |
| Unknown outcome age and escalation | Age starts when an attempt first could have caused an effect without confirmation; ends on evidenced reconciliation, or at report time if still unknown. Report all unknown episodes opened in the window plus older unresolved backlog, median/p95/max age, overdue unresolved count, and episodes escalated by the mandate deadline / all episodes whose deadline fell in the window. Attempt/reconciliation/escalation receipts are evidence; retries never reset the clock and cancellation alone does not resolve it. |
| Lead time | Elapsed time from server-accepted intake to independently verified objective completion (repair: store acceptance and accounting receipt); report median/p95 by intake cohort and stage event times, with open-case age and abandoned/cancelled counts. Wall time includes waiting on people/providers; active work time is separate. Local drafts begin no committed-case clock; record draft delay separately if captured. Reopened cases retain original intake time through final accepted completion. |

The economics/outcomes gate includes this complete scorecard and correction,
exception and recovery costs, not only successful-operation averages. Longitudinal
benefit remains unproven until its declared observation window is complete.

## Delivery dependencies and parallel lanes

The first resumed implementation step is still package 0; this plan authorizes
planning, not that implementation. After migration, UX is the next product
stage. Work that does not commit product implementation can prepare in parallel.

```text
0 Start migration
  └─ 1 Owner-reviewed UX and workflows
       ├─ 1a Early intelligent vertical → informs 2–9
       ├─ 2 Records model and data lifecycle ─┐
       ├─ 3 Authority/evidence contract ─────┴─ 4 Candidate app construction
       │                                         └─ 5 Durable operations
       │                                              ├─ 6 Repair application ─┐
       │                                              └─ 7 Launch application ─┴─ 8 Continuing operator
       │                                                                       └─ 9 Improvement → 10 Full release
       └─ Contract traceability, historical replay fixtures, and market/economics
          measurement design can progress alongside 1–5 without changing runtime.
```

Package 1a proves the intelligence and decision experience before completion of
the generic engine; its bounded prerequisites may overlap foundation design.
Its portfolio reasoning, investigation and reusable tools integrate into packages
8–9 for prospective outcome verification before package 10. Optional neighbouring
product coordination is not a release dependency.

Packages 2 and 3 can proceed as parallel design/implementation lanes once their
shared interface is agreed; their integrated acceptance is sequential. Candidate
construction and durable execution may overlap after their schema/action
contracts are stable. Repair and launch verticals can then be built and accepted
in parallel by separate owners. Package 8 requires both; package 9 requires
operational evidence from 8, and
package 10 requires both loop gates. Evaluation design can proceed earlier, but
reports and dashboards alone cannot satisfy either loop. The held-out third-app
exercise in the full acceptance gate checks that shared foundations serve a
different model and role topology.
